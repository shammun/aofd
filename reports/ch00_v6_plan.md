## 1. The physics the user asked about (verified against the build script's map)

The map: T = 10 °C + x / (100 km), with the pole at x = 300 km, so 13.00 °C. The wind is 10 m/s at 60° to ∇T, so v = (5.00, 8.66) m/s, and
∇T = (1.00 × 10⁻⁵ K/m, 0). The balloon's air parcel is **at the pole at t = 0** (PIN_KM = (300, 180)).

| Question (U-M6.8, U-M6.10) | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is +0.18 K/h? | v·∇T is the speed across the isotherms times the temperature step per metre: 5 m/s × 1 °C/100 km. In one hour (3600 s) the wind carries an air parcel 5 × 3600 = 18 km straight across the isotherms, and 18 km × 1 °C/100 km = 0.18 °C. So **+0.18 K/h is how fast the wind carries an air parcel into warmer places on the map** (a rate in space × a speed = a rate in time). Both routes give the same number: this *is* the advection term. |
| Which air parcel? | The advection term at a place and time belongs to the air parcel that is there at that moment: at the pole at t = 0 that is the balloon's parcel. The wind and ∇T are the same everywhere here, so every parcel has the same +0.18 K/h. |
| Does the wind add 0.18 K to the parcel each hour? (the user's reading) | **Only if the map stays frozen.** Case 1 (thought experiment): something (the sun) keeps every place at its temperature, so ∂T/∂t = 0 and DT/Dt = v·∇T = +0.18 K/h. The balloon then reads the map wherever it goes: 13.00 → 13.18 → 13.36 → 13.54 °C at 1, 2, 3 h (x = 318, 336, 354 km). The rise comes only from moving to warmer places. Case 2 (our story): no sunshine, no mixing, so the parcel keeps 13.00 °C, DT/Dt = 0, and the *map* changes instead. |
| Why "0.18 colder" when the term is positive? | Positive means warmer **ahead** (downwind). So **behind** (upwind) it is colder. The pole is fixed and its new air comes from behind: the air that reaches it in one hour is now 36 km back along the wind = 18 km across the isotherms on the cold side, at 12.82 °C. In Case 2 it keeps that temperature, so the pole falls: ∂T/∂t = −v·∇T = −0.18 K/h. Same size, opposite sign, two different observers. |
| Why multiply the shadow by one hour? | Distance = speed × time. Multiplying 5 m/s by 3600 s gives how far, across the isotherms, the air travels in one hour; the map then turns that distance into degrees. |
| At ~9:40, increasing or decreasing? | Both statements are true for different observers: the moving parcel heads into air that is 0.18 °C warmer per hour (+, the advection term); the fixed pole receives air that is 0.18 °C colder per hour (−, the local rate, in Case 2). v5 said the upwind (−) half before the forward (+) half was established, so the sign looked wrong. v6 shows forward (+) first, then the two cases, then upwind (−). |
